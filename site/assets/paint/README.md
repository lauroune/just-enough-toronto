# Acrylic paint assets

Four distinct transparent acrylic paint textures, made with the built-in image generation tool. The original palette remains: ochre/sienna/coral, forest/sage/petrol, ultramarine/lilac, and vermilion/rose/ochre.

The October 2026 revision replaces bright white specular speckles and crunchy impasto with matte pigment, gentle ridges, and continuous brush grooves. The artwork has varied silhouettes: a long ochre sweep, a tall green gesture, an angled blue stroke, and a compact coral accent.

The assets preserve their natural proportions. Their longest edge is 1440 pixels, encoded as transparent AVIF at quality 65 with 4:4:4 colour sampling. CSS keeps the paint outside the reading column; narrow screens place the long accents above the heading and below the footer. Paint remains static and does not respond to the cursor.

The original swatch loading approach is now retired. `build-paint.mjs` embeds the reading fonts and each complete composition in content-hashed stylesheets. Media queries make only the active artwork stylesheet render-blocking. Paint and type arrive together, with no image reveal animation or JavaScript loading gate.

No white pigment or white glare; highlights stay within the pigment's own colour. Natural, calm, smooth tonal transitions while retaining real bristle texture; not flat illustration, not plastic or glossy CGI. True transparent background and holes, no canvas, no paper, no ground, no cast shadow outside the paint. No text. Single isolated coherent composition with at least 8% clear margin on all edges. Preserve the reference's lively acrylic character and respective colour family. Select the UPPER LEFT ochre/gold/sienna/coral cluster. Re-shape as a long gently rising left-to-right gestural sweep, approx 3:1 width to height, with one honey-ochre stroke, a narrower rusty sienna stroke beneath it and a short restrained coral accent at one tip. Make the strokes different lengths, organic feathered tapered ends. Horizontal landscape composition. Keep all paint inside the canvas.
