## Final prompt

Create a hyperreal fine-art reproduction of an actual physical ACRYLIC PAINT landscape, isolated on a transparent background, intended to frame the central text on a personal website. Wide 3:2 canvas. No text. This is a photograph/scan of real pigment on a flat surface, not a digital painting effect. The artwork is abstract and gestural, but the paint material is photographically convincing.

LAYOUT, ESSENTIAL: A very narrow Toronto skyline with tall CN Tower and small vermilion Toronto streetcar occupies only the leftmost 22 percent, from near the top to the bottom. A grove of three unequal green trees, with a tiny stack of two coloured books at their roots, occupies only the rightmost 25 percent, rising from the bottom to about 20 percent from the top. A long ultramarine/lilac lake or river paint stroke follows ONLY the bottommost 9 percent and connects the bases of both sides. The middle 53 percent of the canvas is ENTIRELY TRANSPARENT above that bottom river: a clear rectangle for a website's real text. Do not intrude any paint or branches into the central rectangle. No writing or UI.

PAINT MUST LOOK LIKE THE REAL THING: An accomplished painter made the scene with professional heavy-body acrylics and a coarse, worn flat hog-bristle brush. Dense opaque pigment, confident broad strokes and gestural abstraction. The flat brush leaves dozens of extremely fine parallel hair grooves inside each broad stroke, different pressure at the beginning and end, streaks of incompletely blended pigment, rounded brush-loaded starts and tapered scrape ends. Rich physical paint body, irregular overlaps between dry and newly applied layers, occasional subtle palette-knife scrape exposing an underlayer. Most strokes are pressed flat, with a raised edge or modest paint build-up. No tube-squeezed ribbons, no piped icing, no exaggerated gelatinous peaks. Long strokes on the CN Tower and water should look like a real loaded brush moved by a hand. Trees are groups of purposeful broad green brushstrokes, not individually rendered leaves.

Photographic studio reproduction: camera exactly perpendicular to a flat painting, absolutely tack-sharp microtexture across the paint, fine pigment granularity visible within marks, realistic small variations of MATTE acrylic reflectance. Opaque substantial acrylic, never watercolor, never translucent washes. No invented canvas texture. No noise overlays. No oversharpened gritty dots. No plasticky 3D rendering or smooth toy-like highlights. A very light binder sheen at most; no bright white glossy reflection tracing the edges.

COLOURS: believable artist pigments, deep pine/forest green, olive and moss-sage trees; ultramarine and muted lavender water; gold ochre, burnt sienna, dusty pink, coral accents; the abstract CN Tower uses indigo, mauve and warm ochre. Rich but NOT neon. The CN Tower must have its iconic high observation pod and antenna.

IMPORTANT SHADOW CONSTRAINT: No cast shadow outside any painted form. No black or grey halo around the observation deck, trunks, streetcar, books or brush edges. Everything beyond the coloured paint is true transparent alpha. Internal surface relief remains subtly visible within the pigment only, without black outlined crevices. Flat soft frontal illumination as if captured on a professional fine-art scanner.

Maximum clean native detail and resolution, preferably 3840 x 2560 or larger. Transparent background. No white canvas, no mat, no frame, no easel, no art tools, no captions, no checkerboard. Elegant, restrained, quietly Canadian, with the tactile reality of an original painted artwork.
